## 3. Skills developed — Reflection page (2:00–3:00)

[滚动到 Skills developed]

These six cards connect each skill to the work that shows it. Three of them show how my skills grew across the degree.

First, interface programming. In Studio Project 1 I built a whole game; in Studio Project 2 I went deep on the UI layer and learned how interface carries game state between screens, rather than just displaying it.

Second, materials and effects. Building effects from material nodes and Blueprint in Technical Art, instead of using presets, was my biggest step — it showed me what the engine is doing underneath.

Third, working under pressure. The game jam taught me to plan, build and finish a whole game in three days, and that most of the work in a team is communication.

## 4. Key learnings (3:00–4:00)

[滚动到 Key learnings]

Across my degree, three things stand out.

One: a good team project needs three things at once — a reasonably rigorous plan, a team where people can talk openly, and everyone understanding and delivering their own part. Lose any one, and the whole game is held back.

Two: agree on boundaries before splitting the work. If people aren't clear where their part starts and ends, work overlaps in some places and goes missing in others — and you usually find out at integration, when it's too late.

Three, on the technical side: materials are the same ideas in different tools. Take a dissolve effect. In Unreal, I compare a noise texture with a parameter and feed it into Opacity Mask. In Unity Shader Graph, the noise goes into Alpha and the parameter into Alpha Clip Threshold. In a hand-written shader, it's one line — clip(noise minus cutoff). Once I understand the idea, I can move it between engines.
